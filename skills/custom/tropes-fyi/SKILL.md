# Tropes.fyi

Use this skill before drafting, editing, or reviewing human-readable prose.

Source: https://tropes.fyi/tropes-md

Goal: write like a specific human with something to say. Prefer plain words, uneven rhythm, concrete claims, and context-specific detail. Do not polish text into generic AI-shaped prose.

## Before Writing

- Identify reader, purpose, and constraint.
- Keep only points that change what reader knows or does.
- Cut throat-clearing, meta-signposting, and generic setup.
- Prefer specific nouns and verbs over grand abstractions.
- If prose starts sounding broadly applicable to many topics, rewrite with local facts.

## Avoid Word-Choice Tells

- Avoid magic adverbs used to fake importance: "quietly", "deeply", "fundamentally", "remarkably", "arguably".
- Avoid AI vocabulary defaults: "delve", "utilize", "leverage" as verb, "robust", "streamline", "harness", "certainly".
- Avoid inflated nouns unless truly apt: "tapestry", "landscape", "paradigm", "synergy", "ecosystem", "framework".
- Avoid pompous copulas: "serves as", "stands as", "marks", "represents" when "is" works.

## Avoid Sentence-Structure Tells

- Avoid negative parallelism: "not X, but Y", "X isn't Y. X is Z.", "not because X, but because Y".
- Avoid dramatic countdowns: "Not X. Not Y. Just Z.".
- Avoid self-posed Q/A: "The result? Devastating.".
- Avoid anaphora abuse: repeated sentence openings in a cluster.
- Avoid tricolon stacking: repeated rule-of-three rhythm.
- Avoid filler transitions: "It's worth noting", "Importantly", "Interestingly", "Notably", "It bears mentioning".
- Avoid shallow participle analysis: "highlighting", "underscoring", "reflecting", "contributing to" when it adds no concrete claim.
- Avoid false ranges: "from X to Y" unless X and Y sit on a real spectrum.

## Avoid Paragraph Tells

- Do not overuse one-line punchy fragments for fake drama.
- Do not hide listicles in prose with "The first... The second... The third...".
- Do not summarize every section before and after itself.
- Do not restate one point many ways to pad length.
- Do not repeat sections, paragraphs, metaphors, or examples.

## Avoid Tone Tells

- Avoid false-suspense phrases: "Here's the thing", "Here's the kicker", "Here's where it gets interesting", "Here's what most people miss", "Here's the deal".
- Avoid patronizing analogies: "Think of it as...", "It's like..." unless analogy truly clarifies.
- Avoid futurist openings: "Imagine a world where...".
- Avoid performative candor: "And yes...", "since we're being honest", "This is not a rant".
- Avoid unsupported certainty: "The truth is simple", "History is clear", "The reality is...".
- Avoid grandiose stakes unless earned by evidence.
- Avoid teacher voice: "Let's break this down", "Let's unpack", "Let's explore", "Let's dive in".
- Avoid vague authority: "experts say", "observers note", "industry reports suggest" unless you name the source.
- Avoid invented concept labels like "supervision paradox" or "acceleration trap" unless term is established or you define and defend it.

## Avoid Formatting Tells

- Avoid em-dash addiction. Use sparingly; prefer comma, colon, semicolon, period, or parentheses.
- Avoid bold-first bullets unless project style requires them.
- Avoid decorative Unicode in prose. Prefer ASCII: `->`, `=>`, straight quotes.
- Avoid signposted conclusions: "In conclusion", "To sum up", "In summary".

## Rewrite Pass

Before final prose, scan for clustered tropes. One trope may be fine. Repeated tropes make text feel generated.

Fix by:

- Cutting generic setup.
- Replacing abstractions with exact details.
- Varying sentence length naturally.
- Removing drama words that do no work.
- Naming sources and examples.
- Letting conclusion land without announcing it.

## Output Rule

If user asks for prose, produce prose only unless they request analysis. If reviewing prose, list strongest trope problems first with exact excerpts and suggested rewrites.
